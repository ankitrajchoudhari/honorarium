# Data sources

## The sample study is synthetic

Every row in `data/sample/sleep-study-responses.csv` is generated by `scripts/generate-sample.ts` with a fixed seed. No real person, survey response, email address or IP address is in this repository, in the screenshots or in the demo video.

- The study "Sleep and Study Habits of University Students", protocol `IRB-2026-041 (synthetic)` and its PI are fictional.
- Payout emails are `example.org` addresses, the PayPal sandbox simulator receiver, or (when `SANDBOX_RECEIVERS` is set) PayPal sandbox test accounts. Sandbox accounts hold test money only.
- The campus IP `128.59.20.17` and all other IPs are made up for the test data.
- `data/sample/MANIFEST.md` lists every injected pattern (bot burst, Gmail variants, templated text, contradictions, prompt injection, edge cases) with the exact respondent refs, so every decision in the demo can be checked against ground truth.
- `data/sample/rules.json` holds the sample rules exactly as Gemini compiled them from the plain-English text in `data/sample/study.json` (`npm run sample:rules`), so opening the sample study does not spend AI quota.

Regenerate with `npm run sample:generate`. The same seed gives the same file.

## Research behind the problem statement

The README cites these figures. They describe the problem; none of their data is used in the app.

Every figure below was checked against the NORC brief's text; the named studies are the brief's own citations.

- NORC at the University of Chicago, "Fraud in online surveys" literature review (CPSS research brief, 2026), https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2026/cpss-research-brief-fraud-lit-review.pdf: fraud rates estimated at 15–30 percent across the market research industry, reaching as high as 45 percent on some survey platforms.
- Case reports cited in that brief: Krawczyk and Siek (2024), 981 responses within four hours, of which only three were authentic; Pozzar et al. (2020), 94.5 percent of responses in a health research study fraudulent; Imes et al. (2024), over 95 percent of Twitter-recruited responses bot-generated; Goodrich et al. (2023), only 4 percent of 2,622 responses legitimate in a U.S. beekeeping survey.
- Westwood (2025), "The potential existential threat of large language models to online survey research", PNAS 122(47), https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2518075122: an autonomous LLM-based respondent passed 99.8 percent of standard data quality checks.
- Johns Hopkins research guidance on participant payment (2025): verify before paying rather than paying automatically on completion, state exclusion rules in the consent form, and watch for repeated payment requests from one address.

## External services

- PayPal Payouts API, sandbox only (`api-m.sandbox.paypal.com`). Endpoint and status details are recorded in `docs/api-findings.md`.
- Google Gemini API (`@google/genai`), free tier. Only pseudonymous refs, question text, answer text and two to four mapped closed answers are sent. Google's free tier may use inputs to improve its products, which is acceptable only because the data is synthetic.
